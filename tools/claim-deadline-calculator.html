How we count days

The start date never counts. Calendar days add whole civil days and are labeled on each row. Business days — and Illinois “working days” — skip Saturday, Sunday, and the 11 U.S. federal holidays in 5 U.S.C. 6103, using the observed date when a holiday falls on a weekend (Saturday holidays observed Friday, Sunday holidays observed Monday).

Notice-based carrier clocks use the date of loss you enter. If notice was given later, use that later date as the date of loss to match the carrier’s clock. Proof-of-loss clocks stay blank until you enter a proof-of-loss date. A statutory floor (Texas and Louisiana suit periods) is shown for comparison and is not a filing date.

Questions, answered.

Is this legal advice?

No. This calculator gives general information about verified state claim-handling rules. It is not legal advice and does not create an attorney-client relationship. Confirm every deadline with a licensed attorney or your public adjuster before relying on it.

Which states are included?

v1 dates verified rules for Florida, Texas, Louisiana, Georgia, Illinois, and South Carolina. Oklahoma, North Carolina, Missouri, and Colorado appear as coming soon. Unverified rules are never shown.

How do you count days?

The start date never counts. Calendar days add whole civil days. Business days and Illinois working days skip Saturdays, Sundays, and the 11 U.S. federal holidays on their observed dates (Saturday holidays observed Friday, Sunday holidays observed Monday).

What if my policy has a different deadline?

Enter the policy deadline in the optional override. The calculator shows both the policy date and the verified statute date, and marks the later date as the deadline that favors the policyholder. Policy language, catastrophe orders, extensions, and court decisions can still change the result.

How often are the statutes checked?

Statutes are pulled quarterly and verified yearly by a licensed firm owner. Each rule lists the last verified date and the next review date.

Why is there no date on some rows?

Some clocks start at proof of loss or at a later event such as notice of acceptance or a written demand. If that date is not on this form, the rule and citation still appear, without a computed date. Unverified rules are omitted entirely.
